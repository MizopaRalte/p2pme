🔌 How Ports & Remote Connections Work
Why multiple users can use the same port without colliding, and how private pairing tokens keep you 100% secure.

1. The "House & Door" Principle:
Think of an IP Address as a street address (e.g. 192.168.1.50 or 100.85.120.44), and the Port (45455) as a specific apartment door inside that building. Even if 1,000,000 users use port 45455, they live in completely different "houses" (different IP addresses), so they will never collide or mix up.

2. Two Servers in the Same Home?
If you run two P2Pme servers inside your home (e.g. an Android phone and a Windows PC), your router automatically gives each one a different local IP address (192.168.1.50:45455 and 192.168.1.55:45455). Devices on your Wi-Fi reach them independently. If forwarding through a router, you can assign custom external ports (e.g. 45455 for Phone 1, 45456 for PC).

3. 256-Bit Security Pairing Tokens:
Every P2Pme receiver generates a cryptographically unique secret token. Even if someone scans your IP and discovers port 45455, all incoming requests without your secret token are instantly dropped.

4. Want Zero Port Hassle?
Use WebRTC Direct (browser-to-browser NAT hole punching) or Tailscale Mesh. Both assign unique cryptographic peer IDs and require zero router port forwarding.
